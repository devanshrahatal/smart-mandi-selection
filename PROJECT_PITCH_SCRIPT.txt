PART 1: THE PROBLEM (2 minutes)
================================================================================

Start by asking the audience a question:

"Let me ask you something. If a farmer in Jaipur grows 20 quintals of tomatoes
and checks Agmarknet — the government's official price portal — he sees that
Azadpur Mandi in Delhi is offering ₹2,901 per quintal, while his local Kota
Mandi is offering only ₹2,450. Which mandi do you think he should go to?"

Pause. Let the audience say "Delhi" or "Azadpur."

"Most people — and most farmers — would say Delhi. Because ₹2,901 is clearly
higher than ₹2,450. But here's the thing nobody tells them..."

"To get to Delhi from Jaipur, he has to hire a Tata Ace truck. That costs him
roughly ₹927 per quintal in transport freight alone. Then there's the APMC
mandi commission — 7.5% in Delhi — that's another ₹218 per quintal. Loading,
unloading, weighing charges — ₹70 more. And because tomatoes are one of the
most perishable crops in India — with a perishability index of 0.85 — after
6+ hours of travel in Indian summer heat, he loses another ₹119 per quintal
to spoilage and quality downgrades."

"So what started as ₹2,901 per quintal... after all these hidden deductions...
becomes just ₹1,568 per quintal in his hand."

"Now let's look at Kota — the mandi he rejected. It's only 250 km away.
Transport is just ₹484. Commission is lower. Spoilage is minimal because
the journey is shorter. His actual take-home from Kota? ₹1,737 per quintal."

"That's ₹169 MORE per quintal from the 'cheaper' mandi. On 20 quintals,
that's ₹3,382 of additional profit he lost — just because he chased a
higher advertised number."

"And it gets worse. If he had gone to Vashi Mandi in Mumbai — where the
price looks attractive at ₹2,820 — the transport alone would have been
₹2,347 per quintal. His net take-home? Zero. An actual financial loss."

"This is what we call 'The Price Illusion.' And it's not a rare edge case.
Over 86% of Indian farmers are smallholders. They operate on razor-thin
margins. One wrong mandi decision can wipe out an entire season's profit."

"Every existing agricultural portal — Agmarknet, eNAM, Kisan Suvidha —
they all show you the gross advertised price. Nobody shows the farmer
what actually matters: the NET take-home profit after all deductions."

"That's the problem we set out to solve."


================================================================================
PART 2: THE SOLUTION (1.5 minutes)
================================================================================

"We built the Smart Mandi Selection Platform — an intelligent decision engine
that calculates real take-home net profit for every candidate mandi within
a 300-kilometer radius of a farmer's location."

"Instead of showing just the raw price, our system applies a 5-factor
mathematical formulation:"

"Net Profit equals Modal Price, minus Transport Cost, minus Mandi Commission,
minus Loading and Handling Charges, minus Transit Spoilage Degradation."

"The transport cost is computed using actual road distances — either from
Google Maps Distance Matrix API or our Haversine formula with an Indian
highway detour multiplier of 1.28x — multiplied by commercial vehicle
freight rates per kilometer per quintal."

"The spoilage deduction is where our innovation really shines. We built a
scientific perishability decay model. Every crop has a decay index — Tomato
is 0.85, Banana is 0.70, Onion is 0.25, Potato is 0.20, Wheat is 0.05.
The spoilage formula is: Modal Price × Perishability Index × (Transit Hours
divided by 24) × 0.15. This means the system automatically penalizes distant
mandis more heavily for perishable crops and less for grains."

"The result? Farmers get a ranked list of mandis — sorted not by who's
advertising the highest price, but by who will put the most money in
their pocket."


================================================================================
PART 3: DOES THIS ALREADY EXIST? WHAT'S UNIQUE? (1.5 minutes)
================================================================================

"Now, you might wonder — there are many agri-tech platforms out there.
eNAM, Agmarknet, Kisan Suvidha, KrishiSense. Do any of them already
solve this?"

"The short answer is: no. Not like this."

"Agmarknet and eNAM show raw modal prices. They don't deduct a single
rupee of logistics. A farmer looking at their data has no idea what
he'll actually earn."

"KrishiSense, which won SIH last year, focuses on crop recommendation
and disease prediction — it tells farmers WHAT to grow, not WHERE to
sell. Completely different problem domain."

"There are a few transport aggregators and logistics apps, but they
don't integrate price intelligence. They'll help you book a truck, but
they won't tell you whether it's worth going to that mandi in the
first place."

"Here's what makes Smart Mandi genuinely unique — five things no other
platform combines together:"

"ONE — We compute Net Profit, not Gross Price. We're the only platform
that subtracts transport, commission, handling, AND perishability-based
spoilage from the advertised price to give the real take-home number."

"TWO — Scientific Perishability Decay Model. Not a flat percentage —
a crop-specific, time-dependent degradation formula that gets worse
with longer transit hours. No one else models spoilage this way."

"THREE — Kisan Pool Shared Freight Optimizer. Instead of each small
farmer hiring their own Tata Ace, our algorithm pools neighboring
farmers' harvests into larger commercial vehicles — Eicher 14ft,
19ft Heavy Trucks, 24ft Multi-Axle — reducing individual transport
costs by 35 to 55 percent."

"FOUR — Zero-App WhatsApp Voice AI. Farmers don't need to download
anything. They send a voice note in Hindi, Marathi, Gujarati, or
English to a WhatsApp number. Our system transcribes it, runs the
full net profit calculation, and sends back both a text table AND
a synthesized voice note reply in their native language."

"FIVE — Interactive GIS Profit Mapping. A Leaflet-based map with
color-coded route lines showing exactly why the nearest mandi might
be more profitable than the distant one — Green for best profit,
Teal for moderate, Amber for suboptimal."

"No single existing platform offers even two of these together.
We offer all five."
